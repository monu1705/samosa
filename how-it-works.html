How the “AI” works (spoiler: heuristics!)

We believe in classroom honesty. Everything runs locally with deterministic checks you can inspect and explain.

Contact scan

  • Regex captures email, phone, LinkedIn, and GitHub URLs.
  • If any field is missing, it’s flagged with a friendly reminder.
  • First non-empty line is checked for a professional name format.

Structure & length

  • Counts words and estimated reading length to spot multi-page resumes.
  • Looks for classic headings like “Experience” and “Education”.
  • Warns when bullet ratios vs. paragraphs look off.

Bullet strength

  • Identifies bullet lines and checks for action verbs up front.
  • Flags vague phrases like “responsible for” or “worked on”.
  • Suggests adding metrics when no numbers are detected.

Grammar & clarity

  • Highlights repeated spaces, common misspellings, and fragment lengths.
  • Checks for inconsistent punctuation at line endings.
  • Encourages parallel voice across bullet sections.

Keyword match

  • Role selector maps to curated keyword lists for each discipline.
  • Missing high-impact keywords are surfaced with add-now suggestions.
  • Counts role-aligned verbs to increase specificity.

Formatting check

  • Detects mixed date formats (Jan 2024 vs. 01/2024) and flags mismatches.
  • Warns when entire sections are in all caps or lengths vary dramatically.
  • Looks for standard bullet markers to keep layout consistent.

Why call it “AI” at all?

The point of this prototype is to show the workflow of an AI helper: upload, analyze, suggest, and export. For a production-grade tool you’d integrate a genuine language model API, but for class we rely on transparent heuristics anyone can read in the code.
